# 📊 GigGuard v2.1 Machine Learning Analysis

This document provides an executive analysis of the trained ML model residing in `/ML_Engine/GigGuard_v2_copy`. This analysis is crucial for demonstrating the actuarial soundness of our pipeline to investors or hackathon judges.

## Model Architecture
*   **Algorithm:** `XGBoost v2.1` (Gradient Boosted Trees)
*   **Target Variable:** `loss_ratio` (The actuarial probability of a disruption resulting in a payout).
*   **Input Space:** 39 total features (combining raw weather, rolling windows, lag features, geographic data, and deterministic trigger states).
*   **Dataset Size:** 126,175 rows spanning 10 years and 35 distinct GPS climate zones in India.
*   **Performance:** Achieves a Test $R^2$ of **0.8795** and Test MAE of **0.0207**, meaning our model can confidently explain 87.95% of the variance in parametric weather payouts without overfitting (Walking Forward CV R² is 0.8808).

## 📈 Feature Importance Breakthrough

Upon analyzing `feature_importance_v2.csv`, we observe a massive breakthrough in our approach: **The model learned the value of our heuristic physics engines.**

Top 10 Most Important Features:

2. `n_triggers_active` (10.3%)
3. `trigger_heat_active` (6.6%)
4. `trigger_flood_active` (6.3%)
5. `rain_squared` (5.8%)
6. `precipitation_sum` (2.7%)

### The "Aha!" 
Notice that the top 4 features are **not** raw weather data—they are the Boolean flags generated by our `disruption_triggers.py` heuristic engine! 

Before feeding data into the `XGBoost` model, our backend uses physics and geo-fencing (like Delhi NCR AQI or Coastal flooding) to calculate boolean triggers. The ML model automatically realized that these structured triggers are **far more predictive** than raw `precipitation_sum` alone. This proves we have built a highly structured, expert-guided ML system, escaping the "black box" trap of pure deep learning.

## 🧬 Feature Engineering Insights

We engineered specific composite features to help the model learn non-linear risks:

*   **`rain_wind_interaction` (1.9% importance):** Multiplying rain by wind mathematically tells the model about "driving rain," which is exponentially harder for gig riders to navigate than rain with no wind.
*   **`zone_safety_score` (0.8% importance):** By feeding elevation and distance to the coast into a heuristic, the model correctly learned to give massive premium discounts to geographically safe landlocked areas compared to high-risk flood zones like Mumbai or Kolkata.
*   **`rain_squared` (5.8% importance):** Rain impact isn't linear. 10mm of rain is slightly annoying; 100mm is devastating. Squaring the variable allowed the model to penalize extreme tails appropriately.

## ⚠️ Tail Risk & Cap Protections
Because machine learning struggles to extrapolate beyond its training data bounds (out-of-distribution tail events), we don't blindly trust the ML model. That's why we implemented the `MIN_WEEKLY` (₹20 floor) and `MAX_WEEKLY` (₹49/₹99 caps) in `compute_dynamic_premium()` to guarantee financial affordability while leveraging the Sigma Risk Load for sustainability in 1-in-100-year weather events.
